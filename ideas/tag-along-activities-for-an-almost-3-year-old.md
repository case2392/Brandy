# Tag-Along Activities for the Almost-3-Year-Old

The goal: whatever big sister is doing at the table, little sister has a
"same but simpler" version so she feels included — not a separate babysitting
plan. This also front-loads her own pre-K readiness for free.

## The parallel-activity pattern

| Big sister (4½–5) is doing... | Little sister (almost 3) does... |
|---|---|
| Letter tracing / handwriting | Same letter in a salt tray, with finger paint, or built from playdough snakes |
| Phonics / letter sounds | Letter hunt: find today's letter on magnet tiles, point to pictures starting with its sound |
| Counting & simple math | Counting bears/buttons into cups, one-to-one pointing, stacking by size |
| Bible story + activity | Sits in for the story, then does the simpler craft version (gluing pre-cut pieces vs. cutting her own) |
| Science / nature activity | Same activity, sensory version — pouring, sorting leaves, magnifying glass time |
| Read-aloud time | Fully shared — read-alouds need no modification at all |
| Independent seat work | "Busy bag" rotation: lacing cards, big-piece puzzles, sticker pages, pom-pom tongs |

## Principles that make it work

- **Same theme, different depth.** If it's Letter B week and the Bible story
  is Noah, *both* girls are doing B and Noah — one traces, one smears paint.
- **A rotation of 6–8 busy bags** (swap contents weekly) covers the moments
  when big sister genuinely needs one-on-one time with Mom.
- **Expect 5–10 minute attention spans** at almost-3, and let her drift to
  free play without it being a failure.
- Note: Heart of Dakota's "Little Hearts for His Glory" is explicitly designed
  for folding in younger siblings — a point in its favor in the curriculum
  trial.
