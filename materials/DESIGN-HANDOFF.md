# Design Handoff — Homeschool Printables

Hand this document to a design-focused Claude session (or any designer). It is
self-contained: everything needed to redesign, restyle, or rebuild the four PDF
printables lives here. No other context required.

## 1. Project context

These are homeschool printables for one family:

- **Teacher:** Brandy (mom, first-year homeschooler)
- **Student 1 ("big kid"):** daughter, almost 5 — learning letters, early
  tracing, counting; can't read yet, so all instructions are read aloud by mom
- **Student 2 ("toddler"):** daughter, almost 3 — tags along with a simpler
  parallel version of whatever big sister is doing
- **Tone:** warm, gentle, Christian. Structured but playful — "couch and
  crafts," not "workbook drill." Nothing should feel like corporate clip art.

Every letter/story is produced in **matched pairs**: a big-kid page and a
toddler page on the same theme, used side by side at the table.

## 3. Content spec (what each page must contain)

### 3a. Big-kid letter page (×26)

1. Header strip: "Letter of the Week" + page-type label; name line
2. Large display pair, e.g. "A a"
3. Tracing row, uppercase: first letter solid (model), 5 dashed hollow
   letters, on handwriting guides (solid baseline, dashed midline, solid
   topline)
4. Tracing row, lowercase: same pattern
5. Letter hunt: ~12 mixed letters, child circles every instance of the
   target letter (both cases; 4 targets seeded among distractors)
6. Framed "Draw something that starts with ___" box (~1/4 page)

### 3b. Toddler letter page (×26)

1. Same header family, different accent color (so mom can tell the two
   apart at a glance in a stack of paper)
2. One **giant** hollow uppercase letter (most of the page) — used for
   coloring, dot stickers, cotton balls, finger tracing. Stroke outline
   only; interior must stay empty/white
3. Small hollow lowercase companion tucked in a corner
4. One row of 3 large dashed letters for crayon scribble-tracing
5. One-line instruction at bottom ("Color it, dot it with stickers, or
   trace it with a finger!")

### 3c. Weekly rhythm chart (×1, landscape)

Left column — "Daily Rhythm (follow the order, not the clock)", 6 blocks:

| Block | Duration | Content note |
|---|---|---|
| Morning Basket | 15 min | Bible story + song + prayer, together on the couch |
| Table Time 1 — Letters | 15–20 min | Phonics/letter of week; toddler: salt tray, playdough letters, busy bag |
| Wiggle Break + Snack | 20 min | Music, dancing, outside |
| Table Time 2 — Numbers | 10–15 min | Counting, patterns, math games; toddler: counting bears, stacking |
| Hands-On Time | 20–30 min | Science, nature walk, craft, or Bible activity — both girls, two levels |
| Read-Aloud + Rest | 20+ min | Books on couch, quiet play while baby naps |

Right column — "Focus of the Day", Mon–Fri; Wednesday is the visually
distinct "Enrichment School Day (3 hrs) — home is just read-alouds"
(placeholder day; must be trivially editable). Plus a "Remember" note:
total seat work ≈ 45 min; everything else is play, books, and life.

### 3d. Bible story sheets (4 stories × 2 levels)

Stories, verses, and per-page content:

| Story | Verse | Big-kid activity | Toddler page |
|---|---|---|---|
| God Made Everything (Creation, Gen 1:1) | "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth." | Color sun/moon/star; draw-your-favorite box | Giant sun, moon, stars to color |
| Noah and the Big Boat (Gen 9:13) | "I have set my rainbow in the clouds." | Color rainbow + ark; circle the pairs; trace "2" | Giant rainbow + ark; cotton-ball clouds craft |
| Jonah and the Big Fish (Jonah 2:2) | "I called to the Lord, and He answered me." | Color fish; trace wavy sea line | Giant fish + waves; tissue-paper waves craft |
| The Lost Sheep (Ps 23:1 / Luke 15) | "The Lord is my shepherd." | Count 5 sheep; trace "5" | Giant sheep for cotton-ball gluing |

Each big-kid story page: header + title + verse + reference, 3–4 sentence
story summary (read aloud by mom), 3 "Let's talk about it" questions, then
the activity art. Each toddler page: title + one-line takeaway ("Jesus loves
ME!"), one giant colorable illustration, a craft idea line at the bottom.
Story text is fixed content — keep wording unless improving age-appropriateness.

## 5. Known weaknesses — where design help is most wanted

1. **Typeface.** Helvetica's double-story `a` and `g` are wrong for
   handwriting instruction. Ideal: a schoolbook/print-manuscript font
   (single-story `a`, simple `g`) for all tracing glyphs — e.g. Andika
   (SIL OFL) or similar openly-licensed print font. Body text can stay a
   clean sans, but friendlier/rounder would suit the audience.
2. **Tracing-letter quality.** Dashed stroke outlines work but real tracing
   fonts have start dots and directional arrows. Even just consistent start
   dots would be a big pedagogy upgrade.
3. **Illustration charm.** The vector art is clean but minimal. Hand-drawn
   warmth (thicker irregular strokes, rounded corners, tiny faces) would fit
   the tone much better. Keep line-art/colorable style — interiors must stay
   white for coloring.
4. **Header system.** Functional but plain; a gentle, consistent brand
   (small motif, softer rules) across all four artifacts would unify them.
5. **Letter hunt** row could be visually richer (scattered in a shape or
   scene rather than a straight row) while staying circle-able.

## 6. Hard constraints

- **US Letter**, standard margins ≥ 0.5"; rhythm chart is landscape, all
  else portrait
- **Home inkjet friendly:** no full-bleed, no heavy ink coverage, no dark
  backgrounds; colorable areas must be white with outlines only
- Must remain legible **printed in grayscale**
- Audience is pre-readers: instructions are for the parent; kid-facing
  elements must work without reading
- Christian content: keep verses and story text; nothing denominationally
  specific
- No stock photos, no clip-art watermarks, no branding other than the
  family's own
- Fonts must be embeddable/openly licensed

- **26 letters × 2 levels must stay template-generated** (don't hand-lay-out
  52 pages); the Bible sheets and chart may be bespoke

## 7. Acceptance checklist

- [ ] All four artifacts regenerate/export at the same paths listed in §2
- [ ] Matched pairs are visually siblings (same theme, obviously different level)
- [ ] Tracing letters use school-manuscript letterforms with guides
- [ ] Grayscale print test passes
- [ ] Enrichment day on the rhythm chart is a one-variable change
- [ ] A 4-year-old can tell "her page" from "little sister's page" by color alone
