# The Good and the Beautiful — Research Notes

**The short answer to "is it Christian-based?": Yes, but with an important
nuance worth knowing about before committing.**

## What it is

- A very popular, beautifully designed, affordable homeschool curriculum
  (language arts, math, science, handwriting, and more)
- Written from a **"general Christian" worldview** — God, prayer, honesty,
  kindness, gratitude, and Bible principles are woven throughout
- Deliberately **non-denominational**: the company states it avoids doctrine
  specific to any particular Christian church
  ([their official statement](https://www.goodandbeautiful.com/pages/does-the-curriculum-include-doctrines-specific-to-any-certain-christian-denomination))

## The nuance

The founder, Jenny Phillips, is a member of the Church of Jesus Christ of
Latter-day Saints (LDS/Mormon). The curriculum itself does **not** teach LDS
doctrine — it's reviewed by members of multiple faiths to keep it generically
Christian. But this is a well-known point of debate in the Christian
homeschool community:

- **Families comfortable with it** say the content is broadly Christian, the
  academics (especially language arts) are excellent, and nothing
  denominational appears in the lessons.
- **Families who pass on it** (mostly evangelical/Protestant) feel that because
  the theology is intentionally kept generic, it can't go deep on things like
  the gospel, salvation, or who Jesus is — and some are uncomfortable
  supporting an LDS-founded company or worry about subtle worldview influence.

## Practical takeaway for us

If we want a curriculum that is *distinctly* Christian — teaching the gospel,
Jesus as Savior, and doctrine, not just biblical values — The Good and the
Beautiful intentionally won't do that, by design. Many families solve this by
using TGTB for academics and adding their own Bible teaching separately;
others prefer a curriculum where the theology is baked in. See
`christian-curriculum-options.md` for alternatives.
